Quick update on EnergyCam.

**140-H is live.** All 93 cameras across the four recorders are connected and
feeding a live occupancy view — 61 rooms, refreshed every couple of minutes.

We ran a verification pass this evening that compares the dashboard against
live camera frames, room by room: 46 of 61 matched exactly, and there were no
false detections anywhere in the building — nothing is counting a poster, a
chair or a reflection as a person. The handful of differences were people
moving between two samples rather than errors.

**What we can see per room today**

- Who is in which room right now, and the total number of people on campus.
- First seen and last seen for every room, every day — when each space
  actually starts and stops being used.
- The full 24 hours, not just office hours. The hours that cost us money are
  the ones after everyone has gone home, so those are the ones we measure.

**Air conditioning — in progress**

Where a unit's display is visible to a camera, the system reads whether it is
actually running. That is being calibrated unit by unit now, with a technician
switching each one off and on so the system learns the difference between the
two states for that specific unit.

Where no unit is in frame, the room is still counted for occupancy — so we
still know when it is empty. The difference is that it flags the room for
someone to check rather than stating the unit's state, and the dashboard says
which of the two it is rather than implying it knows.

**Electricity**

Both K-Electric meters and the solar array are now recorded daily — units
consumed on each connection and units generated by the panels — alongside 17
months of past bills. That lets consumption be compared against occupancy
instead of estimated.

Across those 17 months, 140-H averages about 504 units a day on the two
connections combined. The summer-to-winter difference is roughly 170 units a
day, which is the cooling load and the part this project is aimed at.

Both need confirming with K-Electric before we act on them.
